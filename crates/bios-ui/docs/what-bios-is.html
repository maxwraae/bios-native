Bios is a plasmid and DNA sequence editor that runs in your browser, with an assistant that can read and change the construct you have open. It is free, and there is nothing to install.

If you have used SnapGene or Benchling, the map and the sequence view will feel familiar. The part that is different is the assistant, and most of what is worth learning here is about what it can and cannot do on your behalf.

The loop

Almost everything in Bios is one of four steps, in order:

  1. Open a construct. Drop a file in, fetch a published sequence, or start a blank one. Bios reads GenBank, FASTA, SnapGene .dna, EMBL, APE and plain .seq files.
  2. Change it. Edit bases directly, annotate a span, or ask the assistant to make the change for you.
  3. Order what you need to build it. Bios harvests every primer you have annotated into an order CSV, so the list is derived from the construct rather than typed out again by hand.
  4. Record what came back. Sequencing results are read against the construct you designed, so the file knows whether it worked.

The four pages under Concepts are the vocabulary those steps use. If you read one, read Features: it is the idea the rest of the app is built on.

What the assistant changes, and what it does not

The assistant never edits your construct silently. When it wants to change bases or annotations it hands you a proposal: a card in the chat panel with a summary, the reason, and a base level diff of exactly what would change. Nothing moves until you press Accept.

Reading is different. When the assistant looks something up, translates a region, or searches for a restriction site, it just does it, and you see a small activity pill saying what it looked at. Reads do not need your permission because they change nothing.

Getting the assistant switched on

Signing up is open and the editor works the moment you land. The assistant does not: it stays locked until your account is approved. There is a Request access button in the chat panel, and once you are approved the panel unlocks on its own without a reload.

This is a capacity limit rather than a sales gate. The editor is not a trial and does not expire.

Where your work lives

Constructs live in projects, and a project is the thing you land in when you open Bios. Editing saves as you go, so there is no Save button; press Cmd S if you want to flush a pending save immediately rather than wait the couple of seconds it takes on its own.
